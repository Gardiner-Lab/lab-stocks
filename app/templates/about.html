Lab Stocks {{ version }}

What the lab keeps in its freezers, boxes and shelves: frozen cultures of its plasmids, primers, chemicals, antibodies and plant material. Every tube has a code, a place and a person, and a label you can print. How to use it →

What it holds

Glycerol stocks GL-G-0042

Frozen cultures of the lab's plasmids, each linked to the plasmid it carries: the host strain and system, the selection, the clone number, the biosafety level and permit, the markers, inserts and gene IDs, and where the tube sits in which box.

Primers GL-O-0007

With their sequence, length, GC and melting temperature worked out for you, their direction and the tube they are in. Searchable by any stretch of sequence, so you can tell whether the lab already has one before ordering another.

Chemicals GL-C-0003

With the CAS number, supplier and product number, the room and shelf, the size of the container and its safety data sheet. An Out switch for one that has run out, which puts it on the order list by itself.

Antibodies GL-A-0005

Primary or secondary, the producer and product number, the animal it was raised in, and notes on how it is used in the lab: dilutions, blocking, what worked and what did not.

Plant material GL-M-0012

Leaf DNA, RNA and protein in tubes: what it was taken from, when and by whom, and which experiment it belongs to. A whole box of tubes can be entered in one go.

Orders

Anyone in the lab asks for what is needed; the lab's admin orders it and marks it received. A chemical switched to Out joins the list without anyone asking.

Each kind also takes photos and files, which open in the browser, and a label with a QR code that opens the record on a phone.

Codes

Every record has a code you can write on a tube, say out loud and search for. It starts with the lab's own code, then a letter for the kind of thing, then its number:

GLthe lab Gglycerol · O primer · C chemical · A antibody · M plant material 0042its number in that lab

The lab's lead chooses the lab code before the lab's first record; after that it is fixed, so no two labs ever share a code and a code is never reused. The Seed Bank uses the same lab code for its own records, so GL-S-0001 (a seed lot) and GL-G-0001 (a glycerol stock) belong to the same lab.

With the other tools

  • Plasmid Editor. Confirming a plasmid by sequencing makes its glycerol stock here, with the next number. A stock links back to the plasmid, so you can open its map and its reads from the tube; the editor can tell you which of the lab's primers bind a plasmid.
  • Lab Notebook. Type @ in a page to link a stock. The stock then lists the pages that mention it, so a tube shows what was done with it.
  • Seed Bank. Plant material taken from a plant links to that plant, and a line can reach the glycerol stocks of the plasmid it carries.
  • MoClo Library Tool. The plasmids a stock holds are the ones assembled there.

Who sees what

  • Your lab sees and changes its own stocks.
  • The other labs of your chair group see them, but cannot change them. That is how a shared antibody or chemical is found without asking around. Chair groups are set by an administrator on the dashboard's Labs tab.
  • Other labs see nothing of yours.
  • Orders are seen by the lab's admins; everyone also sees their own requests.

Deleting hides a record rather than erasing it: its code is never given to anything else, and the pages and records pointing at it keep working.

eLabJournal

Where the university also keeps an inventory, Lab Stocks works alongside it rather than replacing it. A lab admin connects the lab to eLab once; after that:

  • Stocks made here are copied to eLab with their box and position, and archived there when they are used up or deleted here.
  • What eLab already holds is brought over once, matched by box and position so nothing is duplicated.
  • ⇧ Update from eLab takes what was changed in eLab since it was last sent, eLab being right where both were changed.
  • In a group shared with other labs, the lab's own people are credited by name for the stocks they made; the rest are held under the administrator with their eLab owner shown on the record, so nobody's work is credited to the wrong person.

Where the data is kept

On the lab's own server, in one SQLite database with the photos and files inside it, backed up each night with the rest of the suite. Nothing goes anywhere else, apart from the copy in eLabJournal when that is switched on.
