How to use Lab Stocks

A stock list is only worth keeping if the tube in the freezer and the record here say the same thing. This page covers the habits that keep them in step, then the steps for each kind of thing the lab stores, printing labels, the order list, and putting mistakes right. What Lab Stocks is →

Golden rules

  1. Write the code on the tube. A tube without its code cannot be found again, however good the record is. Print a label, or write the code on by hand.
  2. Enter it when you make it, not later. The box and position are the part people need most, and the part nobody remembers a week on.
  3. Make a glycerol stock from the plasmid, not by hand. Confirming a clone by sequencing in the Plasmid Editor makes the stock here, already linked to the sequence and its reads. Entering one by hand leaves the tube with no map behind it.
  4. One tube, one record. Two tubes of the same clone in two boxes are two records, so either can be found and used up on its own.
  5. Mark, don't delete. A tube that is finished: set it to used up. A chemical bottle that is empty: switch it to Out, which also puts it on the order list. Deleting is for a record that should never have existed.
  6. Search before you order. A primer can be found by any stretch of its sequence, and the other labs of your chair group are searched too. The lab usually has it already.
  7. Say where it is in words the next person will read. Freezer −80 #2 / box 4 / A3 beats my box.

Finding something

The search box above each list looks at everything on the record at once, not just the name: a code, a plasmid, a clone, a strain, a selection, a place, a supplier. For primers it also searches the sequence — paste 15 bases and it finds every primer containing them, on either strand.

Records belonging to the other labs of your chair group appear marked with their lab, and open read-only. If something you expect is missing, it may belong to a lab outside the group, or have been marked used up: clear the status filter to see those too.

Glycerol stocks

The usual route, which fills most of the record in for you:

  1. Sequence the clone and judge the reads in the Plasmid Editor.
  2. Confirm the plasmid there, naming the clone you confirmed. Its glycerol stock appears here with the next code, linked to the plasmid.
  3. Open the stock and fill in what the bench knows: the host strain and system (DH5α, GV3101), the selection, and where the tube is.
  4. Print its label and freeze it.

For a stock with no plasmid in the editor — a strain from another lab, say — use ＋ Glycerol stock and type the plasmid name as text. Everything else works the same; only the link to a map is missing.

The biosafety level, permit, markers, inserts, gene IDs and donor organism matter for GMO records and for eLab. Fill them in once on the first stock of a plasmid; later stocks of the same plasmid start from the same answers.

Primers

  1. Primers → ＋ Primer. Paste the sequence; the length, GC and melting temperature are worked out as you type.
  2. Give it a name that says what it does (35S-F, tRFP-seq-R), its direction, and the tube it is in.
  3. Primers designed in the Plasmid Editor can be saved straight here, which keeps the sequence exactly as designed.

Before ordering, paste the sequence into the search box. If the lab already has it, you will see it, including in the other labs of your chair group.

Chemicals and safety sheets

  1. Chemicals → ＋ Chemical. Name, CAS number, supplier and product number, the size of the container, and the room and shelf it lives on.
  2. Attach the safety data sheet as a file, captioned SDS. It then shows in its own place on the record, with an Open the SDS button, rather than among the photos.
  3. When a bottle runs out, switch it to Out. It stays in the list, greyed, and is added to the order list so the lab admin sees it needs replacing.
  4. When the new bottle arrives and the order is marked Received, it goes back to in stock.

Antibodies

  1. Antibodies → ＋ Antibody. A short code to write on the tube, whether it is primary or secondary, the producer and product number, and the animal it was raised in.
  2. Put what the lab has learned in Notes about the use: the dilution that worked, the blocking buffer, which fixation ruined it. This is the part that saves the next person a week.

Plant material

  1. Plant material → ＋ Tube for a single sample: what it is (leaf DNA, RNA, protein), what it was taken from, when, by whom, and the experiment it belongs to.
  2. For a whole harvest, use the box entry to make the tubes of a box in one go, then correct the few that differ.
  3. Taken from a plant in the Seed Bank? Name that plant's code, so the sample and the plant point at each other.

Boxes and places

Write the place as a path from the biggest thing to the smallest, and use the same words every time:

Freezer −80 #2/ box 4/ A3

With eLab switched on, the box and position travel to eLab as a real place in its storage, so a position that is already taken there is reported rather than quietly overwritten.

Labels

  1. Tick the records in a list and press Labels for selected, or use Label on a single record.
  2. Choose the label stock you are printing on. The page draws them to size, so print at 100% with scaling off.
  3. Each label carries the code, the name, and a QR code. A phone camera on the QR code opens the record, which is the quickest way to tell what an unlabelled-looking tube is.
  4. With a DYMO printer, the labels can be downloaded as a file to send straight to it.

Ordering

Anyone in the lab can ask for something; the lab's admin does the ordering. A request moves:

to order— the lab admin orders it → ordered— it arrives → received
  1. To ask: ＋ Order request on the Orders tab, or Add to order on a chemical, primer or antibody that already exists. Say how much and why; the lab admin sees it at once.
  2. You can change or cancel your own request until it is ordered.
  3. Lab admins: mark each ✓ Ordered when you have ordered it, and Received when it arrives. A chemical that had run out goes back to in stock by itself.
  4. The Orders tab shows a count of what is waiting, and the requests of the other labs in your chair group are listed with their lab, for their own admins to order.

Photos and files

Any record takes photos and files: a gel of the colony PCR, an order confirmation, a spec sheet, a blot. Drag them onto the record or use Add photos or files.

Photos, PDFs, Word, PowerPoint and Excel files and text files open in the browser when you click them — office files are shown as a PDF — so you can read a spec sheet without downloading it. The original is always there to download beside it.

The notebook and the editor

  • In a Lab Notebook page, type @ and pick a stock to link it. The stock then lists every page that mentions it, under Mentioned in.
  • A glycerol stock's plasmid opens in the Plasmid Editor, with its map and its sequencing reads.
  • From a plasmid, the Seed Bank shows the lines made with it, their seeds and plants.

eLabJournal

For lab admins, once per lab. Under the eLab settings, give the address and an API token, choose which kinds of record to copy and which eLab sample types they match.

  1. Bring over what eLab already holds, once. Records are matched by box and position, so a tube that is in both ends up as one record, not two.
  2. In a group shared with other labs, set the lab's own people: each eLab owner who is one of yours, and the suite account that is theirs. They are then credited for their own stocks; everyone else's are held under the administrator with their eLab owner kept on the record.
  3. After that it runs by itself. Press ⇧ Update from eLab when something was changed in eLab that should come back here.

If a stock will not go to eLab, open it: the reason is on the record. Much the commonest is a storage position already taken in eLab.

Fixing mistakes

  • Wrong box or position: just edit it. The change is logged with your name, and goes to eLab on the next round.
  • Tube finished: set it to used up, which keeps it findable and archives it in eLab. Don't delete it — the notebook pages that used it still point at it.
  • Entered twice: delete the one nothing points at. Deleting hides it; its code is never given to anything else.
  • Credited to the wrong person: for stocks brought over from a shared eLab group, this is what the lab's own people settles. Add the person and their account, and everything of theirs is re-credited at once.
  • Wrong lab code: it can only be changed before the lab's first record. After that, ask an administrator.
  • Not sure what changed: every record has its own history at the bottom, saying who changed what, and when.
